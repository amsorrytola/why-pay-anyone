# M3 findings: adoption in the economy (exploratory)

Built by `pipeline/build_m3.py` (fetch: `pipeline/fetch_indeed.py`). Everything below is exploratory and descriptive. Nothing here is a forecast or a proven cause.

## Data used, and what each file really covers
- **US Census BTOS** (six workbooks used; URR holds response rates and counts and is not used). AI questions: Q7 (firm used AI in any function, last two weeks) and Q28 (expects to use AI in the next six months) have **26 fortnightly periods, 202524 to 202623** (reference periods 3 Nov 2025 to 1 Nov 2026), in every file. Q8 (employees used generative AI), Q9 (effect on headcount) and Q10 (effect on hiring) have **two periods only, 202622 and 202623**: a snapshot. Cycles 202521 to 202523 were not collected. The files word the question "Super Intelligence or SI (formerly AI)". Cells marked S (suppressed) are dropped; `m3_btos_coverage.csv` counts them. Groups: 20 sector codes (19 NAICS sectors plus multi-unit firms), 102 subsector codes, 7 size classes, 53 state codes, 25 metro areas. Complete data for the last four periods exists for 16 sectors, 53 subsectors, 49 states (not AK, WV, WY), 24 metros, all size classes. Estimates are percent of responses; the national figure sits near the small-firm values, so they appear to count firms, not workers (an inference).
- **Indeed AI tracker**: daily share of postings with AI keywords (machine learning, data science, generative AI and similar), 7-day trailing average, 9 countries, Jan 2019 to Oct 2026. India is not covered.
- **Indeed job postings tracker** (CC BY 4.0): index of seasonally adjusted postings, 1 Feb 2020 = 100, daily to 13 Nov 2026. Aggregate files for 11 areas; occupational-category files for 6 countries (US 47 categories, GB 49, AU 47, CA 47, DE 45, FR 34; 53 labels in all). Postings, not hires. Categories come from normalized job titles. Monthly means of complete months (to Oct 2026) are used. Regional files were not downloaded.
- **Exposure**: Microsoft AI applicability score and Anthropic Economic Index (AEI) usage, from `occupations.csv` (BLS employment, May 2025). Each SOC occupation is assigned to one Indeed category by longest matching SOC prefix (a documented judgement, `m3_category_soc_map.csv`); 99% of US employment is mapped. Category exposure is the employment-weighted mean score (`m3_category_exposure.csv`). The AEI ratio is very skewed (catch-all computer occupations), so it is used by rank only. US weights are applied to every country.

## Methods
- **Adoption**: Q7 "Yes" share by group; mean of the first 3 periods against the mean of the last 4; ranks among groups with complete data; standard errors from the published ones, treating fortnights as independent (consecutive fortnights are different panels, so this is fair for window means and conservative for the change over a year). Dispersion is the SD across groups, with a noise-adjusted version (variance minus mean squared standard error). Trend: OLS with Newey-West errors.
- **Event study**: log of each category's monthly index, minus its Jan to Oct 2022 mean; month dummies and month dummies times treatment (top third against bottom third of exposure, middle third dropped, or standardized exposure); errors clustered by category, 95% intervals from t with (categories minus 1) degrees of freedom; permutation p-value (10,000 shuffles of exposure across categories, fixed seed). This matches a two-way fixed effects difference-in-differences (checked: both give -23.8%). Placebo: the same design with a pretend launch in Dec 2021. Robustness: drop software development; drop the four tech categories; AEI rank in place of Microsoft score; employment weights; new postings only; a control for the size of the 2021 to 2022 boom; leave one category out.
- **AI share**: monthly means; AI postings index = share times total postings index, relative to Feb 2020, to separate real growth from a shrinking denominator; log-linear growth before and after Nov 2022 with HAC errors.

## Key numbers
**BTOS (US firms).** Q7 rose from 17.3% (202524) to 25.0% (202623), about 8.2 points a year (95% interval 7.3 to 9.1; a straight line fits, R2 0.96; one year cannot identify an S-curve). Expected use in six months (Q28) is 28.2%. Last four periods by group, with standard errors of 0.3 to 1.8 points: sectors from 47.8% (Information) and 42.2% (Professional, Scientific, Technical) down to 11.6% (Transportation and Warehousing) and 11.8% (Accommodation and Food); size classes from 23.2% (5 to 9 employees) to 42.1% (250 or more), and the gap between 250 or more and 1 to 4 employees grew from 12 to 19 points; states from 38.4% (DC) and 30.5% (UT) to 13.5% (PR) and 13.8% (MS); metros from 34.7% (Charlotte) to 19.2% (New York). Across the 16 sectors the SD is 12.8 points (noise-adjusted 12.7), so the gaps are real, not sampling noise; across states 5.9 (4.4). Sector adoption rank-correlates with category exposure (Spearman 0.66, p = 0.011, n = 14 sectors; AEI 0.75). Snapshot: 6.95% of firms say AI raised headcount in the last six months and 2.45% say it lowered it (net +4.5 points, SE about 0.3); for hiring, 2.4% up and 3.15% down (net -0.75, SE about 0.3). 87% report no change on headcount. The files do not say whether Q9 and Q10 are asked of all firms or only AI users.

**AI share of postings (Indeed).** US share 2.4% (Oct 2022), 3.9% (Oct 2025), 7.6% (Oct 2026), a rise of 3.1 times. Because total postings fell about 30% over the same period, AI postings rose 2.2 times. Oct 2026 levels: CA 19.9%, IE 15.2%, GB 10.2%, US 7.6%, AU 7.2%, DE 6.2%, IT 5.0%, FR 4.9%, NL 3.6%. US trend growth was 20% a year before Nov 2022 (13 to 27) and 42% a year after (27 to 59). Canada's jump (6.2% to 19.9% in a year) is smooth in the data but unexplained; treat the level with care.

**Headline: did postings in AI-exposed categories fall relative to others?** Yes, relative to the Jan to Oct 2022 baseline, but the data do not show that generative AI caused it. In the US, the top third of categories by exposure has postings **23.8% lower relative to the bottom third (95% interval -34.9% to -10.7%; permutation p = 0.002)** over Dec 2022 to Oct 2026; per 1 SD of exposure the gap is -11.5% (-17.4% to -5.1%). By year the gap is -21%, -24%, -26%, -24%: it opened in 2023 and has been stable since late 2023 (tercile index, baseline = 100: high 56 and low 74 in Oct 2026). Robust: drop software -21.5% (-31.9 to -9.6); drop four tech categories -16.3% (-26.0 to -5.4); AEI rank -19.4% (-31.8 to -4.7); new postings -18.2% (-27.6 to -7.6); boom control -24.4% (-35.3 to -11.8); leave-one-out range -25.5% to -21.1%; employment-weighted per SD only -6.1% (-11.5 to -0.2), so big, low-exposure categories move less. Other countries, top against bottom third: AU -30%, CA -27%, DE -22%, FR -16%, GB -10% (interval -21% to +3%).

**Timing problem.** The gap started opening in mid-2022, before the launch of ChatGPT (30 Nov 2022). Change in the gap from Jun to Nov 2022: -11.4% (-17.6 to -4.7); from Nov 2022 to Nov 2023: -16.4% (-24.9 to -7.0). The intervals overlap, so no break at the launch can be claimed. Before that, high-exposure categories were gaining (placebo +7.6%, interval -0.7 to +16.5), which looks like a boom and bust in office and tech hiring. Likely confounders: interest-rate rises (from March 2022), normalization after the pandemic hiring boom, and tech layoffs (from late 2022). With 32 clusters in the tercile design, intervals are approximate.

## Limits
One year of BTOS with a wording change; the Do-not-know share jumped 3.9 points and No fell 4.7 at 202622 (Yes +0.75) when Q8 to Q10 appeared, and national standard errors doubled; the cause is not stated. Subsector codes changed to NAICS 2022 at 202616 (flagged in the names). Indeed measures postings, not hiring or employment; its AI-keyword share depends on wording. Exposure scores measure observed use (Copilot, Claude), not job loss. The sector link uses a rough crosswalk from Indeed categories to NAICS sectors, not the true occupation mix (needs BLS industry data, not used). Sector adoption against the US postings change is only weakly related (rho -0.51, p = 0.07, n = 13). India is not in any M3 source.

2. **Who adopts**: `m3_btos_adoption_summary.csv` (`geo_type`, `geo_name`, `first3_mean`, `last4_mean`, `last4_ci_lo`, `last4_ci_hi`, `rank_last4`). Default: sectors, latest, sorted. Small table `m3_btos_dispersion.csv` (`sd`, `signal_sd`).
3. **Hiring effect snapshot**: `m3_btos_snapshot_employment.csv` (question_id 9 and 10; answer Increased, Decreased, Net; `mean_pct`, `se_pct`). Label "two periods only".
4. **AI share of postings**: `m3_indeed_ai_share_monthly.csv` (`ai_share_pct` or `ai_postings_index`); table from `m3_indeed_ai_share_summary.csv`. Default: US, GB, CA, share; toggle share against postings index.
5. **Exposure map**: `m3_category_exposure.csv` (`ms_exposure`, `ms_tercile_us`, `mapping_note`); sector scatter from `m3_btos_sector_exposure.csv` (`ms_exposure`, `q7_last4`) with rho from `m3_btos_exposure_correlation.csv`.
6. **Postings by exposure group**: `m3_event_study_group_paths.csv` (country US, spec tercile_ms; `group`, `month`, `postings_index_baseline_100`), line at 2022-11-30, baseline shaded.
7. **Event study**: `m3_event_study_coefs.csv` (`month`, `pct`, `pct_lo`, `pct_hi`). Controls: country (default US), measure (Microsoft score, AEI rank), design (tercile default, continuous), drop software or tech, new postings, placebo on or off.
8. **Headline card and robustness plot**: `m3_event_study_summary.csv` (window post_all; rows by `spec` and `country`; also the `change_*_launch` rows for the timing point). Always show the interval and the timing caveat.

## Concepts illustrated
Diffusion curve (adoption by firm type); event study and difference-in-differences (baseline, parallel trends, placebo, clustered errors, permutation test); labour-demand signals (vacancy postings, stock against new postings, AI share against total postings); exposure scores and why they are not job-loss forecasts; sampling error, suppression and noise-adjusted dispersion.
